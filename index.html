The model

TVAC is built on a fixed added value formula

TVAC is not “AI vibes” — it uses a conservative five-factor scoring manual with explicit assumptions, so your team can understand what drives the verdict and how to improve the design.

What the five factors capture

  • New Opportunities: distribution, liquidity pathways, new investor access, programmability.
  • Cost Savings: operational efficiency, automation, simpler processes over time.
  • Risk Reduction: improved controls, transparency, settlement/counterparty improvements (when real).
  • Tokenization Costs: build/integration, legal/compliance work, vendor/platform dependencies.
  • New Risks: regulatory friction, governance complexity, tech/custody risks, operational attack surface.
Core formula
Added Value = (New Opportunities + Cost Savings + Risk Reduction) – (Tokenization Costs + New Risks)

Under uncertainty, TVAC scores conservatively and makes assumptions explicit — so you can iterate and re-run variants.

How it works

A conservative workflow in four steps

Designed to be useful early — before heavy legal/vendor work begins — and structured so stakeholders can trust the logic.

Step 1

Describe the case

Asset, jurisdictions, investor scope, liquidity plan, custody model, constraints, and objectives.

Step 2

TVAC evaluates added value

Scores the case on five factors and applies the formula: (NO + CS + RR) − (TC + NR).

Step 3

You get a verdict + a deep report

A decision-grade, case-specific report (15–20 pages, ~6,000 words) with rationale, assumptions, blockers and levers.

Step 4

Iterate (or add human review)

Run variants and compare outputs. Optional human review adds sanity-check notes and clarifying questions (typically 24–48h).

FAQ

Answers to the hard questions

TVAC is decision support: conservative, structured, and explicit about assumptions and uncertainty.

Is TVAC just another AI tool that turns gut feel into a polished report?

No. TVAC is not an AI oracle and it does not claim objective truth. It is a structured evaluation framework that assesses a specific tokenization design against fixed criteria across five factors: value drivers and tradeoffs.

The AI helps explain the outcome, but the verdict is driven by the framework and your structured inputs—not free-form prompting.

If TVAC is “information only,” why should anyone trust the assessment?

Because the method is consistent and explainable. TVAC uses a standardized scoring manual with clear score anchors, tied to case parameters (jurisdictions, investor scope, disclosure, custody/settlement, transfer restrictions, etc.).

You can see what moved the score up or down—and improve the design iteratively.

Can we audit the outcome—what exactly drove the verdict and scores?

Yes at decision level: inputs → criteria → factor scores → key drivers → verdict. TVAC is built for transparency and comparability across cases.

For institutional users, we can add method/version identifiers and structured driver tables to support governance and internal documentation.

Does TVAC replace legal, financial, tax, or investment advice?

No. TVAC provides informational decision-support only. It helps structure the discussion and highlight conditions and tradeoffs, but it is not advice.

Jurisdiction-specific matters must always be validated with qualified professionals.

If AI writes parts of the report, how do you avoid “confident nonsense”?

TVAC is designed to reduce model risk by anchoring output to a fixed evaluation structure and structured inputs, and by forcing explicit assumptions, uncertainty, and “conditions to proceed.”

The text explains the framework-driven assessment—it is not allowed to invent the assessment.

What cases is TVAC best suited for—and when is it not a fit?

TVAC is most useful when “does tokenization add net value?” is genuinely non-trivial: private company equity tokens, funds/private credit structures, receivables, infrastructure/energy projects, multi-jurisdiction offerings, and designs where compliance/transfer/custody/settlement choices shape feasibility.

If you want a tool that rubber-stamps every idea—or you are tokenizing something with no real value delta—TVAC is not for you.

What inputs do we need for a high-quality report?

At minimum: asset type, target investor scope, key jurisdictions, desired token rights, distribution model, disclosure approach, custody/settlement setup, transfer restrictions, and your objective (capital raise, liquidity, operational efficiency, etc.).

The more precise your constraints and design choices, the more precise the assessment and “conditions to proceed” become.

How do you handle data security and confidentiality?

TVAC is built around data minimization. We recommend avoiding sensitive identifiers in the free-text case description.

For the model call, we use the OpenAI API; customers remain in control of what they submit. For highly sensitive use cases, we can discuss stricter handling and enterprise options where eligible.

Why subscribe instead of having our team write internal Word memos?

Because governance and consistency matter. TVAC standardizes evaluation across teams and time, makes cases comparable, and produces a committee-ready explanation with clear “conditions to proceed.”

Word memos are often one-off, hard to compare, and depend heavily on who wrote them.

How do updates affect consistency over time?

We treat the evaluation method as a versioned system. When the scoring manual or logic changes materially, we can label outputs with a method/version identifier so results remain interpretable over time.

For institutional workflows, we can support controlled updates and change documentation.

What do we actually receive when we order a TVAC assessment?

A structured deep assessment report with a decision-grade verdict, factor scores, rationale, key blockers, improvement levers, and practical next steps (length varies by case complexity).

If you request an optional human review layer, we sanity-check the output and highlight potential blind spots—typically within 24–48 hours.

Are you responsible for the outcome or decisions made based on TVAC?

No. TVAC is informational decision-support only and does not assume responsibility for decisions, implementations, or outcomes.

Use TVAC as part of a governance process alongside qualified expert validation.
