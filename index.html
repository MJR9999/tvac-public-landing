How it works

A conservative workflow in three steps

Designed to be useful early — before heavy legal/vendor work begins.

1) You describe the case

Asset, jurisdictions, investor scope, liquidity plan, custody model, constraints, and objectives.

2) TVAC evaluates added value

Scores the case on five factors and applies the formula: (NO + CS + RR) − (TC + NR).

3) You get a verdict + a deep report

A decision-grade, case-specific report (15–20 pages, ~6,000 words) with rationale, assumptions, blockers and levers.

Optional: deeper assessment

Where relevant: risk register, mitigation roadmap, stakeholder alignment, and “what-if” design paths.

FAQ

Answers to the hard questions

TVAC is decision support: conservative, structured, and explicit about assumptions and uncertainty.

How is TVAC different from “just asking ChatGPT”?

TVAC is not a generic chat. It applies a fixed five-factor rubric and returns a structured outcome: factor scores, verdict logic, explicit assumptions, and concrete levers.

The goal is repeatability and comparability across cases — so decisions can be defended internally and improved iteratively.

What do I get in Quick Verdict vs Deep Assessment?

TVAC is self-serve: you can run an evaluation and get the output in minutes (typical runtime ~80–120 seconds, depending on load).

Quick Verdict gives you a fast, decision-grade snapshot: verdict (Go / Borderline / No-Go), the 5 factor scores, top blockers, top design levers, and a short rationale.

Deep Assessment adds the full, case-specific report (typically 15–20 pages / ~6,000 words) with assumptions, value pathways, regulatory friction points, a risk register and mitigation focus, stakeholder alignment, and “what-if” design options that show how the project can increase added value.

If you choose optional human review, we sanity-check the output, highlight potential blind spots, and may ask 1–3 clarifying questions — typically within 24–48 hours.

How can TVAC score added value without all the numbers?

Many early tokenization decisions are made under uncertainty. TVAC makes that uncertainty explicit: it lists assumptions, highlights missing inputs, and scores conservatively based on what is provided.

You can then improve the score by improving the design (or adding missing facts) and re-evaluating.

Is TVAC legal, tax, or investment advice?

No. TVAC provides informational analysis based on your inputs and a defined rubric. It is not legal, financial, investment, or tax advice.

Use the output to structure internal decisions — and validate jurisdiction-specific matters with qualified professionals.

What kinds of cases is TVAC best suited for?

TVAC is most useful where the “added value question” is genuinely non-trivial: private company STOs, fund structures, credit/receivables, infrastructure/energy projects, and other situations where distribution, settlement, governance, and compliance shape feasibility.

It is less useful for “tokenize everything” ideas where there is no clear delta vs conventional rails.

What inputs should I provide to get a high-quality report?

At minimum: asset type, jurisdictions, target investors (retail/professional), custody model, liquidity plan (if any), regulatory posture (assumptions), and the business objective.

The more concrete you are about constraints and distribution, the more precise the friction analysis and recommendations become.

Can TVAC be used as a repeatable framework across my pipeline?

Yes — that’s a core point. The rubric is fixed, which makes outputs comparable across cases and time.

Teams can use it to standardize screening, focus diligence on the right unknowns, and document why a case was paused or advanced.

How fast is TVAC — and what does 24–48h refer to?

The automated evaluation is self-serve and typically completes in minutes.

The 24–48h timing only applies if you request optional human review (a sanity check + clarifying questions, plus a more polished executive summary if needed).
